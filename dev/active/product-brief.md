# Product Brief — Curio

> **Name (2026-08-19): the product is Curio.** A curio is a small object kept because it is worth
> keeping, and a curio cabinet is where a collection is displayed — which is what this product is.
> The name belongs to the platform, never to a brand on it: Curio issues the cards, each house
> signs them.

## 1. Definition

Curio records the luxury purchase experience as digital cards, making the act of collecting cards itself an ongoing brand experience.

Every purchase issues a **digital card**; repeat purchases build the customer's own Luxury Collection. The digital card is issued by scanning the receipt QR code after purchase. A card is not merely proof of purchase — it is the core medium connecting purchase records, product information, authenticity, ownership, sharing, and rewards.

**Curio is a multi-brand platform, not one brand's own app.** Any participating luxury house issues cards through it, and a customer's collection holds cards from several houses side by side. Curio supplies the shell — issuing, collecting, passport, rewards — while each brand supplies its own identity: name, colours, monogram, artwork, and the rewards it hosts. Onboarding a brand is a matter of adding its data, never of rebuilding the product. MCM is the demo brand used throughout this document; read every "brand" below as "any brand on the platform".

## 2. Target

Overseas MZ travelers who shop luxury in Korea and share the experience on SNS.

## 3. Problem

Luxury customers want the brand relationship to continue after purchase, but the experience usually stops at checkout. What was bought, where, and when fades over time, and product, warranty, and care information is scattered across channels. Brands likewise need a lasting touchpoint that drives return visits.

> Turn a one-off purchase into a card that carries forward into the next brand experience.

## 4. Core Feature — Digital Card

Curio centers on a single object: the card. One is issued per purchase; customers keep and collect them, accumulating their brand history.

A card carries brand, product image, product name, collection, purchase date, purchase region, store, product number, and serial. Its detail view extends to material, country of origin, warranty period, care instructions, and repair/care service links.

## 5. Card Types

All cards are **free**, and their design can be customized.

| Card | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Basic Card** | Default card for regular purchases. Records product, purchase, warranty, and care info |
| **Limited Edition Card** | Exclusive design for limited-edition products, emphasizing season/region/collab scarcity (e.g. Seoul Exclusive) |

## 6. Digital Only

> **Scoped out (2026-08-19): there is no physical card.** An earlier version of this brief issued
> a physical collectible alongside the digital one and had customers pick it up in store. That
> half is removed — the card exists only in the app.

The digital card carries the whole product: purchase history, product info, collection management,
SNS sharing, and warranty/repair links. The sense of ownership has to come from the card object
itself — issuing, flipping, customizing, collecting — rather than from a thing in the hand.

## 7. My Luxury Collection

Cards accumulate and are managed like a photo album. Customers filter by **brand**, season, product category, collection, or purchase region, and can group cards into their own collections (e.g. Seoul Collection, 26SS Collection, My First MCM).

Because the collection spans houses, brand is a first-class filter rather than a label: a customer can read the archive one house at a time, or read all of them together as a single record of what they collect.

Not a purchase history list — a personal archive that visually replays the time spent with these brands.

## 8. Personalization & SNS

Custom Cards let customers reflect their own purchase experience. Rather than free-form image generation, customers combine brand-approved colors, patterns, and artwork so brand identity holds.

Finished cards can be shared to SNS, where purchase proof and personal collection sharing happen at once — natural brand exposure.

## 9. Digital Passport

The card extends into a post-purchase management tool:

- Product and serial lookup
- Warranty period
- Material and care instructions
- Official repair/care service links

This removes the need to keep paper warranties and purchase records separately — purchase through care lives in one card.

## 10. Card-Based Rewards

No stamps. **Cards held** are the unit of customer activity.

Reaching a card count or completing a specific collection unlocks non-retail items, limited rewards, or brand events (e.g. a non-retail item at 5 cards; a limited perk for completing a season collection).

**Rewards belong to a brand, not to Curio.** Each house sets its own thresholds and hosts its own pickup, so progress is counted per brand — a card from one house does not advance another's reward.

Rewards are designed for in-store pickup where possible, so the reward itself becomes a reason to visit again.

## 11. Experience Loop

Purchase → digital card issued → keep & customize → repeat purchase → collection grows → reward threshold met → store revisit → additional purchase → new card

The point is to convert a customer journey that used to end at checkout into a brand experience that loops around the card.

## 12. Expected Brand Impact

- **Continuity**: the card keeps brand and customer connected past checkout
- **Reach beyond one house**: a customer who joins for one brand's card meets the others already in their collection
- **Collecting**: desire to own extends from products to cards and collections
- **Revisits**: rewards and collection completion give a reason to return
- **Differentiation**: moves beyond spend-based membership to accumulated brand experience
- **SNS reach**: card sharing doubles as purchase proof and brand exposure
- **Aftercare**: warranty, care, and repair info consolidated into the card

## 13. Core Message

> Buy a product and a card remains; stack the cards and the customer's own brand experience is complete.

Purchase → Card → Collect → Reward → Revisit → Repurchase
